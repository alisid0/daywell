# Daywell "Why Daywell": script for approval

27 October 2026. A film of 2 minutes 58 seconds (just under the 3-minute limit on Instagram Reels and YouTube
Shorts), vertical 1080 × 1920, for TikTok, Reels, Shorts and the website. It says why Daywell is needed, in your own
words from the case you set out on 23 October, backed by research you can check, and shown in today's app.

Nothing has been made yet: no voice, no music and no film. Everything waits for your approval of this script.

## The idea

**The question the film answers:** why do good intentions slip, and what helps?

**One line:** everyone wants to do well, and we all know the basics. So we start, and a few weeks later it slips.
Not because we don't care, but because life gets busy and nothing helps us plan it, keep track of it or see that
it's working. Daywell is that help, and if you miss a day, that's fine.

**The claim:** Small, steady steps, planned your way. It's your launch ending, and it's the last line of the film.

**Signature move: the day square.** One rounded day square, in the app's sticker look, is the shape that never cuts.
It sits in a planner month. First it fills up with to-dos, then it empties as the month slips, then it grows into
the phone when Daywell arrives, and at the end it shrinks back into a new month. That new month has small things on
most days and quiet empty days between them, with nothing marking them as failures. No numbers, no counters and no
chains, because Daywell has no streaks or scores.

**DECISION:** Daywell arrives at about 0:50, not in the first ten seconds. A "why" film earns its answer. The Daywell
sun sits small at the top of the first frame, so viewers know whose story it is from the start.

**DECISION:** the launch ending, not the vision ending. Under the 8 October general-wellness scope, a public film
makes no promise about a long life. The vision ending can go in a separate cut for investors or the website's About
page, if you want one.

## Never

- Never call Daywell a dating app. "A date with yourself" is self-care wordplay on daywell.date.
- Everyday wellness only: no promise to improve mental health, treat a condition or extend life (the 8 October
  scope). None of the library's 14 "needs your decision" findings, which mention death, disease, life expectancy or
  blood sugar.
- No stress, anxiety or mood claims, and no condition names in claims.
- No invented numbers, ratings, user counts or reviews; no streaks, scores, counters or progress bars, including the
  planner month.
- Nothing that isn't built: no reminders or notifications, no weekly look-back across all four areas, no research notes
  inside the app, no live voice. The assistant isn't in this film.
- No stock faces and no other company's logos or screens.
- Post only when everything shown works on real phones in the store apps.
